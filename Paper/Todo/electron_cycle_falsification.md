# Remaining research: attempting to falsify the electron-cycle hypothesis

Copyright AStarship <https://astarship.net>.

## Scope and present verdict

This checklist is based on the 46 existing project Markdown files read before making the
animation, the current author's clarification about incoming photons, and the reference
sources below. The pre-production file inventory is `../../Sim/Video/source_inventory.json`.
The animation is `../../Sim/Video/gradient_relativity.py`; it does not solve an electron field.

**Present status: proposed mechanism, not experimentally established and not yet specified
enough for a unique numerical prediction.** The existing scalar-wave pipeline does not
validate electron structure, spin, an incoming-photon driver, or a black-hole-center model.

A scientifically productive question is:

> Can a quantitatively specified model in which incoming photons drive an electron's
> proposed space-energy/mass-energy cycle survive conservation, stability, and existing
> measurements—and what result would exclude that model?

This is an attempt to falsify the positive hypothesis, not to “disprove that electrons do
not oscillate.” Failing to exclude a model would not prove it. If an internal interpretation
has no different observable consequences, it cannot be distinguished experimentally from
the standard description by that test.

## Separate the claims

**Reference physics.** Atomic systems can undergo photon-driven excitation; this does not
by itself establish an internal space-to-mass conversion.[9] A classical event horizon
captures light because of its causal structure; electron absorption is not required to
make a horizon trap light.[1] Local Lorentz covariance applies in regular infinitesimal
regions of GR spacetime.[8] In a local orthonormal frame a null interval obeys
`0 = -c² dτ_local² + dℓ_local²`, so the measured light speed is c; coordinate speeds are not
that local measurement. This last equation is a mathematical consequence of the stated
reference metric, not an observation of the black-hole center.

**Author's postulate.** An internal electron degree of freedom exchanges two proposed
fractions: 100% space at phase 0°, 100% mass at 360°, and 100% space at 720°.
Incoming photons are proposed as a possible driver. These fractions need operational
measurement definitions; they are not established electron phases.

**Speculation.** A mapping from a “maximum expansion” endpoint to a zero endpoint at the
black-hole center. The meaning of zero and the relation to radius, energy density, local
clock readings, and cosmological expansion have not been derived. A singularity prediction
may indicate the limits of classical relativity; it is not evidence for this mechanism.[1]
The center is not the event horizon. The author's latest clarification explicitly proposes
slowing/stopping internal motion there; this must be tested as an additional hypothesis,
not inferred from a coordinate clock or treated as an observation of zero local c.

**Clarified spatial mechanism (author's postulate).** The highest space/EM-energy state
is BETWEEN masses: 100% oscillation/expansion, normalized to 1. Positive pressure is
proposed to push space into both masses, condensing the intervening region and shortening
their separation, which the author identifies as gravity. Those flows point away from
the midpoint but are not proposed as repulsive forces on the masses. The space/EM
component falls toward 0% at the center; energy may remain stored as mass. Neither a
solved pressure tensor nor a measurable geometry/conversion law has yet been supplied.

**Clarified transfer and restart (author's postulate).** Energy could statistically
transfer from any electron to any other through infinitely many possible paths until
a least-action outcome. A proposed majority of galactic electrons inside the hole supplies
central receiving endpoints. A further interaction hits a stopped configuration and
restarts space oscillation. These are not derived from the reference amplitude sum or
from a retrieved electron census. The UNM lecture provides the amplitude/interference
baseline.[11] NASA describes a star cluster surrounding the central hole, not an interior
electron population.[12] Source-linked Markdown context is in `../../Reseach/README.md`.

## What the cartoon assumes—not what it discovers

For visual clarity only, the video chooses

`σ(θ) = [1 + cos(θ/2)] / 2`,
`E_space = σ E₀`, `E_mass = (1 - σ) E₀`, `0 ≤ θ ≤ 4π`.

This produces the requested endpoints, holds the chosen internal total constant, and
repeats after 720°. It is an interpolation, not a derived equation of motion, a conserved
stress-energy tensor, or a spinor representation. A changing σ is not necessarily a
changing observable electron mass. If photon energy enters, the internal total generally
needs input/output terms rather than being silently held fixed.

## Prioritized research tasks and rejection criteria

### R01 — Define quantities before assigning percentages

- [ ] Define space energy and mass energy: local densities, integrated energies, or another
      quantity? Name their units, observer, spatial region, and gauge dependence.
- [ ] Define the internal phase θ, its clock (preferably proper time), its evolution law,
      and how θ differs from spatial orientation and the spatial gradient coordinate g.
- [ ] State whether measured invariant mass changes or only an internal decomposition does.
- [ ] Define “100% expansion” and “zero at the center”; neither is an SI energy or an
      expansion rate merely because its numerical normalization is one or zero.

**Gate:** another researcher must be able to calculate the same observable from the same
state. If the fractions cannot be operationally defined, do not present the mechanism as
an experimentally distinct physical theory yet.

### R02 — Resolve the light-speed conflict and the endpoint behavior

- [ ] Keep `σ = E_space / (E_space + E_mass)` consistently. Do not reverse it near the center.
- [ ] Use a dimensionless ratio `u = C_local / c₀`. An energy-density ratio is dimensionless;
      equating it directly to a dimensional SI speed squared is a unit error.
- [ ] Compare the two existing postulates with the same σ:
      `u² = σ/(1-σ)` and `u = 2√[σ(1-σ)]`.
- [ ] Decide which is the physical proposal and which, if either, is a numerical proxy.
      A different variable requires an explicit invertible mapping, not just a new label.
- [ ] Explain zero or infinite proposed characteristic speeds and whether the equations
      remain causal, hyperbolic, and well-posed at the endpoints.

**Finding from the definitions:** P1 diverges at σ→1 and is not maximal at σ=1/2; the bounded
form vanishes at both endpoints. They agree at σ=1/2, and also at σ→0 in the limiting sense,
but differ elsewhere in the interior. Simply fixing dimensions does not fix these physical
contradictions. If no consistent law implements the intended endpoints, reject that law or
revise the specified model before fitting data.

### R03 — Write an action and derive conservation, rather than asserting it

- [ ] Specify the new field's degrees of freedom and couplings to a charged spinor, the
      electromagnetic field, and the metric. State the intended low-energy domain.
- [ ] Derive equations of motion, electric current, and stress-energy from an action.
- [ ] Demonstrate charge conservation and local total stress-energy conservation including
      radiation, the new field, matter, and the relevant interactions.
- [ ] Check boundedness of energy, ghosts, instabilities, causality, and the GR/QED limits.
- [ ] Distinguish local covariant conservation from a globally conserved energy in an
      arbitrary evolving cosmological spacetime.

**Reject a variant** if it needs untracked energy, momentum, or charge, has a physical
negative-energy instability, or fails its stated reference limit. The current scalar
source and “Planck spring” terminology do not provide these derivations.

### R04 — Derive the incoming-photon interaction, including recoil

- [ ] Choose the environment: bound atoms, a plasma, an external field, or isolated electrons.
- [ ] Calculate allowed transitions or scattering amplitudes, cross sections, and momentum
      exchange. Identify any new excited electron state or recoil partner explicitly.
- [ ] Keep horizon crossing separate from local interaction events.
- [ ] Define what the proposed internal phase does while the charge remains conserved.

**Analytical check (derived here):** an isolated standard free electron initially at rest
cannot completely absorb a real photon and remain an electron of the same invariant mass.
Energy conservation gives `E_final = m c² + Eγ`; momentum conservation gives
`p_final = Eγ/c`. Thus

`E_final² - p_final² c² = m² c⁴ + 2 m c² Eγ`,

which cannot equal `m² c⁴` for a positive-energy photon. This excludes that isolated,
same-mass absorption channel, not bound-system absorption or Compton scattering. A new
excited charged state with different invariant mass would be a different hypothesis and
would need a spectrum and experimental constraints. A new field carrying recoil must be
included in the conservation calculation, not used as an unnamed escape hatch.

### R05 — Calculate the new photon budget and distinguish it from the old driver

- [ ] Choose a local photon spectrum, density, angular distribution, interaction cross
      section, residence time, and independently constrained environment.
- [ ] For photon-number spectral flux `Nν` in photons/(m² s Hz), calculate
      `P_abs = ∫ Nν σ_abs(ν) hν dν` in watts. Use a cross section derived for R04's channel.
- [ ] Track emitted, escaping, and transferred energy and any observer-dependent work term.
      Internal redistribution is not a new energy supply.
- [ ] Show whether the proposed cycle is a driven state, a persistent stable excitation,
      or an initial condition. Derive damping, relaxation, and photon-flux dependence.

**Existing approximation, independently recomputed from `Sim/simlib/constants.py`:** the
old galactic Larmor budget has `P = 3.417151748747211e-73 W` and a per-orbit/rest-energy
ratio `2.634315739062051e-44`. This is negligible for forming an electron from that budget.
It is conditional on using that classical Larmor approximation; gravitational coordinate
acceleration is not automatically the local proper acceleration of an antenna charge.
The curved-spacetime radiation problem needs its own treatment. This old number is **not**
a calculation of the incoming-photon driver and does not exclude it.

**Reject a specified formation/maintenance variant** if its required power exceeds the
available power after losses under its stated conditions. Separate formation from
maintenance before choosing a power threshold; a conservative stable state need not be
continuously re-created once per cycle.

### R06 — Derive spin-1/2 and retain charge through the entire cycle

- [ ] Reproduce the SU(2) spinor transformation law, magnetic coupling, and appropriate
      fermionic statistics—not merely a 720° cartoon or an orbital vortex.
- [ ] Account for charge -e in the allegedly “pure space” phase and electromagnetic response
      in the “pure mass” phase. Define what exactly is absent at each 100% endpoint.
- [ ] Explain whether θ is a physical population exchange or a quantum amplitude phase.

A spinor changes sign under 360° rotation and returns after 720°.[7] For an isolated pure
state a global sign represents the same physical ray; relative signs are meaningful in
interference. Derive the distinction explicitly. A classical scalar's winding number or
two energy bars does not supply a spinor representation or Fermi statistics.

**Reject the identification with an electron** if the model yields only an integer-spin
orbital excitation, loses electric charge at an endpoint, or predicts statistics that
contradict the specified electron baseline.

### R07 — Obtain a stable, finite-energy electron state and calculate mass

- [ ] Solve for a localized state without prescribing its circular motion or choosing its
      measured mass as an input and then claiming that mass as an output.
- [ ] Calculate the total rest-frame energy, relevant self-energy/renormalization, extent,
      magnetic moment, and response to a probe.
- [ ] Test stability to small perturbations, damping, radiation, and background changes.
- [ ] State which parameters are fitted and reserve independent observables for testing.

The measured electron rest-energy equivalent is 0.51099895069(16) MeV in the retrieved
2022 CODATA value.[3] Matching it by setting the oscillator frequency from `m_e c²/ℏ`
is a parameter choice, not a mass derivation. Distinguish angular Compton frequency
`ω = m_e c²/ℏ` from ordinary frequency `f = m_e c²/h`, and reduced wavelength
`ℏ/(m_e c)` from ordinary wavelength `h/(m_e c)`.

**Reject a variant** if no finite stable state exists in its advertised regime or if
independently predicted mass/response misses the reference measurements outside the
predeclared uncertainty.

### R08 — Build standard-physics benchmarks before adding new physics

- [ ] Reproduce a bound-system photon transition and free-electron scattering with recoil.
- [ ] Reproduce a standard spinor rotation and relative-phase interference calculation.
- [ ] Reproduce photon capture and local measurements in a regular horizon-crossing chart.
- [ ] Derive any actual local-c variation in physical dimensionless observables; do not
      infer it from coordinate velocity or a choice of units.

**Pipeline gate:** failure of a known case disqualifies the calculation as evidence. It
is not evidence that the physical hypothesis is false; repair the implementation or
state the approximation's domain first.

### R09 — Replace imposed motion with validated, self-consistent numerical dynamics

- [ ] Begin with an analytical or manufactured solution and a low-dimensional, declared
      effective model. Do not promise to resolve a macroscopic domain at the Planck length.
- [ ] Check stability limits, spatial and time-step convergence, boundary reflections,
      spectrum normalization, and energy/current residuals.
- [ ] Compare photon-driven, no-photon, and QED/GR-baseline runs at identical physical times,
      initial conditions, resolutions, and metric extraction settings.
- [ ] Measure whether a cycle emerges without directly imposing its phase or trajectory.
- [ ] Validate rotating/chiral modes separately; their existence would still not establish
      spin-1/2 or an electron.

**Audit needed:** `Sim/Video/input_report.json` preserves a reported
`m0_power_fraction = 5.54468702308607`, which exceeds one. Its normalization/name must be
resolved before using that diagnostic as a power fraction. Do not convert its existing
PASS label into a physics conclusion. The animation deliberately does not present these
scalar-wave spectra as electron evidence.

**Reject a numerical claim** if it disappears with refinement or is explained by forcing,
reflection, aliasing, non-conserved quantities, or incompatible comparison times.

### R10 — Produce one quantitative, distinguishable laboratory prediction

- [ ] Calculate the model's correction to an electron magnetic moment, spectral line,
      scattering response, or interferometric phase, with parameter dependence and units.
- [ ] Choose the most sensitive channel after calculating the effect—not before.
- [ ] Include standard absorption, heating, Stark/Zeeman shifts, recoil, line broadening,
      plasma effects, and instrumental drift as confounders where relevant.
- [ ] Blind or reserve data; predeclare the model family, fit parameters, statistical test,
      confidence level, and a correction for multiple tested observables if needed.

The retrieved Fan et al. measurement reports a 0.13-ppt magnetic-moment measurement
uncertainty and about 1-in-10^12 Standard Model agreement; it explicitly notes uncertainty
from discrepant independent fine-structure-constant measurements.[4] These are different
quantities. Do not use the measurement uncertainty as a universal new-physics exclusion
bound, or use alpha inferred from the same g measurement as independent confirmation.

**Exclusion rule:** publish the predicted signal and reject parameter regions incompatible
with the measured likelihood at the predeclared confidence level. A null result with too
little sensitivity says “not resolved,” not “disproved.” No unique experimental threshold
can be calculated until the model supplies its parameters and observable map.

### R11 — Decide what can actually test the black-hole-center extension

- [ ] Specify a spacetime/background and whether the proposed new field changes the metric,
      matter response, photon propagation, or all three.
- [ ] Compute an accessible exterior consequence if the interior is causally hidden:
      e.g. a derived modification to accretion spectra, timing, or lensing—not an assertion
      that absorption alone explains a black shadow.
- [ ] Fit ordinary astrophysical/plasma uncertainties and match a GR baseline.
- [ ] State the domain where classical predictions fail; do not extrapolate an observer's
      coordinate clock or a static radial profile through a singularity as a measurement.

The retrieved NASA explanation distinguishes horizon capture from the unknown physical
interpretation of the central singularity.[1] If a proposed center has no accessible
consequences, external observations cannot select it among other hidden-center models.
**Verdict in that case: presently untestable extension, not confirmed.**

### R12 — Synchronize the paper, evidence, and independent review

- [ ] Replace historical assertions with consistent definitions across abstract, summary,
      equations, and public copy; identify superseded text without silently deleting history.
- [ ] Keep established results, postulates, illustrative ansatzes, numerical checks, and
      measured findings in visibly distinct categories.
- [ ] Publish input/source hashes, solver versions, controls, convergence checks, raw results,
      uncertainty calculations, and rejected preliminary interpretations.
- [ ] Ask a QED specialist and a GR/curved-spacetime specialist to review specific equations
      through `https://github.com/AStarStarship/GradientRelativity/issues`.
- [ ] Defer strong-force replacement, dark-matter replacement, and cosmological claims until
      their respective standard baselines and quantitative predictions are recovered.

**Acceptance gate:** independent reproduction of a distinguishable prediction, followed by
comparison with data. An appealing video, a successful renderer, or an unknown black-hole
center does not satisfy this gate.

### R13 — Test the clarified pressure, contraction, transport, and restart mechanism

- [ ] Distinguish space/EM amplitude, energy fraction, energy density, expansion rate,
      pressure in SI units, and total energy. Derive rather than equate these quantities.
- [ ] Specify a conversion rate or interaction term for space-to-mass condensation and
      the compensating stress-energy/current balance. Name the matter and field states.
- [ ] Derive the change in an operational separation (proper distance, radar distance,
      or another stated observable). Coordinate relabeling or a shrinking cartoon fails
      this gate. Positive pressure by itself is not an equation for that separation.
- [ ] Compare with matched GR and appropriate matter/field stress. Recover the specified
      weak-field force and observational limits before attributing the contraction to gravity.
- [ ] State the claimed galactic electron fraction, epoch, composition, uncertainties,
      and meaning of an electron inside the proposed interior. Present-day majority and
      an eventual concentration endpoint are different hypotheses; do not swap them.
- [ ] Supply source/receiver states and a transport action or kernel. Calculate received
      flux/probabilities rather than assuming receiver counts select a destination.
      Compare with amplitude interference and causal response in the reference theory.
- [ ] Define the motion that stops: local translation, internal oscillation, angular motion,
      or another variable, with a clock/observer. No known highest atomic level is implied
      by calling the proposed mass component maximal.
- [ ] Derive an incoming-hit restart including input energy, recoil, losses, charge, and
      any redistribution of stored energy. Compare hit and no-hit controls at equal times.
- [ ] Keep regenerated space/EM activity inside the assumed interior distinct from an
      accessible emitted photon; do not assert an outward horizon-crossing signal in GR.

**Reject a specified variant** if its pressure/conversion law fails conservation, supplies
no real change in the chosen separation, predicts an excluded mass/population/response,
or requires a restart that is only programmed into the trajectory. If the interior has
no accessible predicted consequences, retain an explicitly untestable extension rather
than treating it as confirmed. These are proposed gates, not results of completed tests.

## Smallest useful next experiment

First complete R01–R04 enough to choose a definite interaction. Then pre-register an
effective model with a fixed parameter set, a monochromatic photon input, and a clear
control with identical electrons and no photon input. Derive the standard QED result
for that same environment. Choose an accessible response observable after calculating
its magnitude and uncertainties. Only then run the matched calculation and assess whether
a cycle emerges independently of an imposed phase. The deliverable is a predicted response
curve and an exclusion region—not another movie with the desired oscillation programmed in.

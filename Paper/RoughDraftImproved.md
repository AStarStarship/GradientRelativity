# On the Electrodynamics of Gravitational Equilibriums

![Gradient Relativity overview](./Gradient%20Relativity.png)

The overview image is retained from the earlier notes as a schematic, not measured evidence.

**Author:** Cale McCollough  
**Status:** Working research draft; clarified mechanism, not a validated physical theory.

## Abstract

Gradient Relativity proposes that mass is condensed space. In the author's spatial picture,
the highest space/EM-energy state lies between masses: 100% oscillation and maximum
expansion, normalized to 1. Proposed positive pressure drives space into both masses.
Although the flows point away from the intervening region, the intervening space contracts,
reducing the separation; the author identifies this contraction as gravity, not a repulsive
force on the masses. A separate proposed electron cycle exchanges space and mass components
across a 360-degree + 360-degree internal cycle. The transport proposal connects possible
electron sources and receivers through infinitely many possible paths and a least-action
interpretation. At a speculative black-hole center, the space/EM component approaches 0%,
internal motion slows, energy is stored in the mass component, and an incoming interaction
restarts space oscillation. Zero space/EM activity does not mean zero total energy. This
draft distinguishes these postulates from reference physics, imposed animations, and
recorded numerical approximations. A pressure/conversion law, operational distance model,
conserved field dynamics, electron properties, and quantitative observable predictions
remain to be derived.

## 1. Introduction and scope

The starting hypothesis is that a spring-like or "Planck-field" substrate can exchange what
the author calls space energy and mass energy. A condensed configuration is proposed to
manifest as matter; expansion and oscillation correspond to the space/EM component. These
names do not yet specify an action, a measurable energy density, or a pressure in SI units.

The proposal aims to connect gravitational contraction, electron structure, and photon-driven
energy transfer. It is not presently a derivation of general relativity or quantum
electrodynamics (QED). The distinction between the following categories applies throughout:

- **Reference physics:** the relativistic energy-momentum relation, photon/matter coupling,
  quantum amplitudes, and the difference between a black-hole horizon and its center.
- **Author's postulates:** condensed space, the spatial endpoints, pressure-driven contraction,
  the internal cycle, proposed transport, and the center's storage/restart mechanism.
- **Implemented illustrations:** prescribed phase fractions, moving markers, and finite
  path fans. They show the intended story without solving its dynamics.
- **Recorded numerical approximations:** legacy classical budgets and scalar-wave runs under
  their stated input and speed-law choices. They are not observations of electron interiors.

The clarification supersedes the earlier inference that galactic centripetal acceleration
by itself establishes a self-sustaining electron whirlpool. The original
[RoughDraft.md](RoughDraft.md) remains historical notes rather than the current manuscript.

## 2. The spatial gradient: pressure into masses and gravitational contraction

### 2.1 The 100% endpoint between masses

In the author's proposal, the highest space/EM-energy state is in the intervening region
between two masses. Space can oscillate at 100%, described as maximum expansion and
normalized to 1. This is the proposed spatial endpoint, not a measured local light speed,
Hubble expansion rate, or established vacuum energy density.

A two-body Newtonian force-balance point provides a useful reference geometry. For idealized
point masses, ignoring other forces and the rest of the galaxy,

\[
\frac{G M_1}{d_1^2}=\frac{G M_2}{d_2^2},
\qquad d_1+d_2=r.
\]

This reference balance does not determine the proposed space/EM component. Nor does it
establish that a finite intervening region has zero net force, that an electron there must
radiate, or that the local vacuum speed of light reaches a special maximum.

### 2.2 Why the two flows are not proposed as repulsion

The proposed positive pressure pushes space INTO both masses, where it condenses into mass.
Viewed from the midpoint, the two flows point in opposite directions, away from the middle.
The author identifies the outcome with loss or contraction of the intervening space: the
separation shrinks. In this account, gravity is this contraction, not a repulsive force
pushing the two masses apart.

A drawing can impose inward motion and shorten a ruler. That is an illustration of the
postulate, not an operational distance calculation. A completed model must specify the
pressure/stress, conversion rate, matter response, and geometry, then predict a change in
proper distance, radar distance, or another declared observable with its observers and
measurement procedure. A coordinate rescaling alone is insufficient.

Positive-pressure arrows do not derive an attractive acceleration or an inverse-square law.
The proposed dynamics must recover the appropriate weak-field behavior and distinguish
contraction from the effects already predicted by a matched GR/matter-field model.

### 2.3 Distinguish spatial labels, internal fractions, and propagation speed

Three quantities must not be equated merely because each has a normalized value:

| Quantity | Intended role | Unresolved relationship |
|---|---|---|
| Spatial label \(g_s(x)\) | 1 between masses; toward 0 at the proposed center | Operational amplitude, fraction, or rate |
| Internal fraction \(\sigma(\theta)\) | Space component of an electron-cycle illustration | Coupling to the spatial field |
| Speed ratio \(u=c_{\mathrm{model}}/c_0\) | Candidate propagation law relative to reference vacuum \(c_0\) | Local, coordinate, or effective-medium speed |

The spatial normalization and internal bookkeeping are different concepts in the current
story. Their connection must be derived. Naming them separately is not a reparameterization
that automatically repairs incompatible speed laws.

## 3. Incoming photons and the proposed electron cycle

### 3.1 Reference interactions and an unspecified new coupling

A bound system can absorb a photon into an allowed excited state.[9] Scattering, recoil,
complete absorption, and causal trapping are different processes. An isolated free-electron
absorption proposal must satisfy energy and momentum conservation; a driven mechanism must
identify the interacting system and any recoil partner.

The new claim is that an incoming interaction can drive or restart conversion between space
and mass components. The coupling, its selection rules, and the conserved charged state
have not been specified. Calling an endpoint "pure space" does not turn an electron into a
neutral photon or remove the need to account for its charge.

### 3.2 A 360-degree + 360-degree internal illustration

The proposed internal sequence is:

1. A space-dominant endpoint, illustrated as 100% space component.
2. Conversion toward a mass-dominant endpoint across the first 360 degrees.
3. An incoming interaction at the stored-mass endpoint, followed by renewed space activity
   and a return across another 360 degrees.

For the animation only, let \(\theta\) be an internal phase in radians and use

\[
\sigma(\theta)=\frac{1+\cos(\theta/2)}{2},
\qquad 0\leq\theta\leq4\pi.
\]

At \(\theta=0,2\pi,4\pi\), this imposed curve gives space fractions \(1,0,1\), with mass
fractions \(0,1,0\). It is one interpolation, not an equation of motion or a measurement of
an electron's phases. The animation's time schedule does not determine a physical frequency.

For a closed, unforced bookkeeping example with a declared positive total \(E_0\),

\[
E_s=\sigma E_0,\qquad E_m=(1-\sigma)E_0,
\qquad E_s+E_m=E_0.
\]

For a driven control volume, input, output, mechanical work, and losses must instead be
included. If \(P_{\mathrm{in}}\) and \(P_{\mathrm{out}}\) denote all net energy transfers,
a bookkeeping balance is

\[
\frac{d}{dt}(E_s+E_m)=P_{\mathrm{in}}-P_{\mathrm{out}}.
\]

This identity is not a derived conserved stress-energy tensor. The model must independently
account for momentum, angular momentum, and charge during both condensation and restart.

### 3.3 What the older oscillator approximation establishes

A dimensionally ordinary oscillator template is

\[
m_e\ddot{x}+k_{\mathrm{eff}}x=F_{\mathrm{drive}}(t),
\qquad \omega_0=\sqrt{k_{\mathrm{eff}}/m_e}.
\]

The field that supplies \(k_{\mathrm{eff}}\), the force, damping, and nonlinear response
remains unknown. Setting a frequency equal to a Compton reference scale is an identification,
not a derivation of electron structure.

The older approximation inserted a galactic centripetal driving scale. A galactocentric
coordinate trajectory does not by itself specify a radiating charge's proper acceleration,
its force, or radiation boundary conditions. An emission calculation must declare these.
For a constant-force oscillator template, \(x_p=a_c/\omega_0^2\) is a displaced equilibrium
particular solution; it does not automatically drive a persistent rotating internal mode.

The frozen record in `../Sim/Video/input_report.json`, under
`validation.feedback_budget`, gives the assumed classical Larmor power as approximately
\(3.42\times10^{-73}\,\mathrm{W}\), energy per assumed galactic orbit as
\(2.16\times10^{-57}\,\mathrm{J}\), and the ratio to electron rest energy as
\(2.63\times10^{-44}\). These are recorded approximation results, not a new simulation
performed for this revision and not a photon-driver budget. The specified galactic estimate
cannot supply the rest energy on that timescale. The incoming-photon proposal needs its own
spectrum, cross-sections, receiving states, losses, and energy budget.

### 3.4 Mass and spin remain derivation targets

NIST lists the electron rest-energy equivalent as \(0.51099895069(16)\,\mathrm{MeV}\) in
the source retrieved for this project.[3] Repartitioning a fixed internal total does not
by itself make the observable electron rest mass vanish during part of a cycle.

The reference spinor transformation changes sign under a 360-degree rotation and returns
after 720 degrees.[7] A two-turn cartoon is not a derivation of spin, the magnetic moment,
Fermi statistics, or a stable localized electron. The proposed field must reproduce these
properties rather than assign them to a trajectory after the fact.

## 4. Speed-law consistency, units, and the Planck scale

### 4.1 Two candidate laws are not equivalent

The older draft's ratio ansatz is dimensionless only when written for a declared normalized
speed. Under the internal-fraction definition above, Postulate P1 becomes

\[
u_{\mathrm{P1}}^2=\frac{E_s}{E_m}=\frac{\sigma}{1-\sigma}.
\]

The scalar FDTD prototype instead uses the bounded choice

\[
u_{\mathrm{bounded}}=2\sqrt{\sigma(1-\sigma)}.
\]

Both give 1 at \(\sigma=1/2\) and approach 0 as \(\sigma\to0\). P1 diverges as
\(\sigma\to1\); the bounded form goes to 0 there. Consequently, neither the equal-fraction
point nor the author's spatial 100% endpoint can be silently declared a shared maximum of
both laws. The conflict is unresolved. Treat the formulas as different candidate models
until a valid relationship and measurable meaning have been established.

A location-dependent locally measured vacuum speed would be a departure requiring its own
consistent spacetime/field theory. It must not be presented as ordinary GR local invariance
merely because every observer uses a symbol \(C(x)\).[8] Coordinate speed, material-medium
speed, and a local vacuum measurement are separate notions.

### 4.2 Keep the reference energy-momentum relation separate

The reference relation is

\[
E^2=(m c_0^2)^2+(p c_0)^2.
\]

At rest, \(E=m c_0^2\); for a massless photon, \(E=p c_0\). Replacing \(c_0\) everywhere
by an undeclared field-dependent value is not a derived extension. A modified dispersion
relation and the detector's measured rest energy must follow from the proposed dynamics.

Choosing units in which a reference speed is 1 requires consistent changes of units; it
does not permit substituting the number 1 into an otherwise SI expression. Likewise, the
spatial label \(g_s=1\) is not a speed in metres per second or a pressure in pascals.

### 4.3 Planck-scale and scattering limits

The reference Planck-length expression is

\[
l_P=\sqrt{\frac{\hbar G}{c_0^3}}.
\]

A units convention \(c_0=1\) does not make this length zero. Substituting a hypothetical
variable speed into the expression is a further postulate, not evidence for an observed
infinite length at a black-hole center. A relation to Hawking temperature or an upper bound
on a proposed spring length has not been derived here.

Similarly, scattering formulas require a specified medium, frequency/wavelength convention,
material response, and constants held fixed. The older inference from a classical electron
radius to a universal Rayleigh \(C^{-4}\) transparency law is not retained as a prediction.
Rayleigh scattering does not by itself supply a black-hole horizon or the proposed interior
population.

## 5. Electron-to-electron transport and possible paths

### 5.1 The author's infinite-path interpretation

The transport proposal treats any electron as a possible source or receiver of energy
through the field. The author describes movement through infinitely many possible paths
until a least-action outcome. This is the intended interpretation to investigate, rather
than a completed transfer kernel, propagation calculation, or measured trajectory.

The finite fan of curves in the video represents this proposal. It does not evaluate an
infinite functional integral or show a photon sequentially trying an infinite list of
routes before choosing a receiver.

### 5.2 Reference amplitudes and stationary action

In the retrieved nonrelativistic path-integral teaching baseline, propagation is described
by amplitudes with the phase \(\exp(iS/\hbar)\); interference depends on the sum of complex
amplitudes.[11] Schematically,

\[
\mathcal{A}=\sum_{\gamma}\mathcal{A}_{\gamma},
\qquad P\propto|\mathcal{A}|^2.
\]

The reference stationary-action approximation is not a rule that always picks a global
minimum or the shortest geometric route.[11] A photon/electron interaction or a curved-
spacetime calculation requires appropriate field variables, source and receiver states,
causal response, coupling, recoil, and boundary conditions. Counting possible endpoints
alone does not derive received energy flux or transition probabilities.

### 5.3 The central-electron population is an assumption

The author proposes a central majority of galactic electrons inside the black hole as a
statistical receiving population. This draft records the claim as an assumption, not an
observed electron census or a consequence of the reference amplitude sum. Its epoch,
composition, meaning of an electron in the proposed interior, and predicted fraction must
be specified. A present-day majority and an eventual concentration endpoint are different
claims and must not be substituted for each other.

The retrieved NASA source describes a dense star cluster surrounding the Milky Way's
central black hole.[12] Surrounding stars are not a count of interior electrons. The
proposed population and transfer bias must be confronted with a declared mass/composition
budget and a calculated, accessible prediction.

## 6. The proposed black-hole center: slow, store, and restart

The author's proposed inward progression is from the spatial 100% space/EM endpoint toward
0% space/EM at the black-hole center. The zero label applies to that component; it does not
mean zero total energy. The stored mass component can remain large.

At this endpoint the author proposes that electrons slow toward zero in an internal
motion or oscillation variable while being excited into a maximal mass-energy storage
configuration. This is not a known highest atomic level. The motion, local clock, charged
state, and energy allocation still need operational definitions; neither a coordinate clock
nor an observer-dependent slowing is an observation of the proposed mechanism.

A further incoming interaction hits the stopped configuration and restarts conversion into
space energy and oscillation. The animation imposes this restart. A physical model must
include the input energy, any release of stored energy, recoil, losses, charge continuity,
and stability; a no-hit control must be evaluated over the same physical time.

The center is not the event horizon. NASA's reference description separates the horizon's
causal trapping from the unknown center structure and identifies the singularity prediction
as a possible limit of classical relativity.[1] Incoming capture need not be electron
absorption. The proposed restart is not evidence for an outgoing photon crossing a standard
GR horizon from the interior. Zero proposed EM activity is also not a measurement of zero
local vacuum light speed or zero gravitational interaction.

If the interior extension supplies no accessible consequences, it remains an explicitly
untestable extension rather than a confirmed explanation of observed black-hole behavior.

## 7. Candidate observables and falsification

The present draft does not supply numerical predictions for an anomalous magnetic moment,
new spectral line, additional redshift, gravitational shielding, or a cosmological signal.
Those are calculation targets, not demonstrated consequences. Precision electron magnetic-
moment measurements illustrate a possible comparison channel, with reference-theory and
input-constant uncertainty included.[4]

The highest-information next steps are:

1. **Pressure and separation:** specify a two-body geometry and distance measurement;
   derive the pressure/conversion response rather than program the contraction. Compare
   with the matched GR/matter-field baseline, including the weak-field limit.
2. **Driver budget:** specify incoming radiation and receiving states; calculate net stored
   energy, recoil and losses. Compare photon-driven, no-photon, and standard QED controls.
3. **Electron properties:** derive a stable charged state and its response, including spin,
   magnetic moment, scattering and interference. A prescribed periodic scalar wave is not
   a fermion calculation.
4. **Transport and population:** calculate flux/probabilities for specified sources and
   receivers; constrain the proposed galactic concentration rather than assume it as data.
5. **Center restart:** define the stopped variable, hit/no-hit controls and an accessible
   predicted consequence while respecting the declared causal model.

Declare numerical inputs, times, averaging windows, uncertainties, and exclusion criteria
before comparing variants. Reject a specified variant if it fails conservation, has no real
change in the chosen distance observable, or predicts an excluded response. A software
check or a null experiment does not settle every conceivable hidden mechanism.

See [the falsification roadmap](Todo/electron_cycle_falsification.md) for the acceptance
ladder and the clarified pressure/contraction/transport/restart gates in R13.

## 7b. Open Problems

1. **Operational definitions and action.** Define fields, dimensions, material/charged states,
   pressure, energy density, and conversion terms. Write the action and stress-energy tensor.
2. **The spatial/internal/speed dictionary.** Derive the connection between \(g_s\),
   \(\sigma\), and measured propagation. Resolve P1 versus the bounded prototype without
   silently changing the meaning of an existing variable.
3. **Gravity from the proposed conversion.** Derive operational separation and acceleration,
   recover the relevant GR limit, and quantify any distinguishable residual.
4. **Driver energy and losses.** The recorded \(2.63\times10^{-44}\) galactic feedback ratio
   does not sustain an electron rest energy per orbit under that approximation. Calculate
   the new incoming-interaction budget; do not reuse the old number as a test of it.
5. **Fermionic structure and observables.** Derive charge, spinor transformations, stability,
   statistics, and detector responses. Assigning a 720-degree trajectory does not suffice.
6. **Transport law and population.** Supply amplitudes or a transfer kernel, causal response,
   source/receiver states, and a constrained electron population.
7. **The center extension.** Define the motion and clock, mass storage and restart coupling,
   and which exterior observations could test the interior proposal.
8. **Numerical integrity.** Require known cases, matched physical times, conservation checks,
   resolution/timestep sensitivity and uncertainty. Separate solver checks from physics.

## 8. Conclusion

Gradient Relativity proposes a specific picture: expansion/oscillation between masses,
space flowing into matter, contraction of the intervening space as gravity, and an internal
space-to-mass cycle with incoming-interaction restart. The center and transport extensions
are explicit proposed dynamics rather than observations. The current manuscript explains
that picture and its falsification requirements; it does not yet derive electron mass,
spin, a gravitational force law, or a replacement for QED/GR. The next step is a conserved
quantitative model that turns the illustrations into measurable predictions.

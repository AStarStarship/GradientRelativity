# Gradient Relativity: A Hypothesis Under Test

Narration: synthesized, not the author's recorded voice.

## Gradient Relativity

Please like and subscribe if you want to follow open research into Gradient Relativity. This is a hypothesis under development, not an established explanation of nature.

Find the paper and code at github dot com slash A Star Starship slash Gradient Relativity. Here is the idea, its boundaries, and how we could test it.

## Three different knowledge levels

First, established physics. Light carries energy and momentum. It can transfer energy to matter, and a black hole can capture incoming light.

Second, Cale McCollough's postulate: an electron might have an internal cycle exchanging what the model calls space energy and mass energy.

Third, speculation: connecting that cycle to a gradient from maximum expansion to a zero endpoint at a black hole's center. The center's physical structure is unknown.

## Between masses: the 100% space/EM endpoint

In McCollough's theory, the highest energy state is between two masses. Space there can oscillate at one hundred percent: maximum space and electromagnetic activity, described as expansion, normalized to one.

That expansion creates proposed positive pressure, pushing space into the masses. As space is condensed into mass, the model's space and electromagnetic component falls from one hundred percent toward zero percent at the black hole. This is the author's mechanism, not a derived pressure law.

The space flows toward both masses, away from the middle, but in this proposal it is not pushing the masses apart. As intervening space condenses into the masses, the distance between them shrinks. McCollough identifies this contraction as gravity. The animation illustrates that claim; it does not derive the measured contraction.

Zero percent refers to the space and electromagnetic component, not zero total energy. Energy can remain stored in the mass component. This spatial gradient and an electron's internal cycle are different concepts; the theory still needs to derive how they are connected.

## Incoming photons: what transfers energy?

The proposal focuses on incoming photons as a possible driver. But crossing an event horizon does not require absorption by an electron. Causal trapping and absorption are different processes.

A bound atom can absorb a photon into an allowed excited state. A free electron can exchange energy through scattering. Complete absorption by an isolated free electron in vacuum cannot conserve both energy and momentum.

So the new step is not that light interacts with matter. It is the claim that this interaction drives a space-to-mass cycle. That needs an explicit coupling, recoil partner, and calculation.

## Electron-to-electron transfer and possible paths

The proposed picture connects electrons through the electromagnetic field. Energy could statistically transfer from any electron to any other electron. The source, receiver, and the possible routes all belong to the story.

McCollough describes the movement as exploring infinitely many possible paths until it takes the path of least action. These drawn curves stand for that interpretation; they are not a calculation of an infinite path integral or measured trajectories.

The proposed inward bias assumes that most electrons in the galaxy are inside its black hole, providing many receiving endpoints there. That majority is an assumption, not an established galaxy census. Observed central star clusters surround the black hole; they are not inside it.

The standard quantum reference sums complex amplitudes over possible paths. Interference leads to stationary action in a classical limit, not necessarily a global minimum. It does not establish a photon sequentially trying routes. The proposed transfer law needs its own probabilities, interactions, and causal constraints.

## The proposed 360-degree + 360-degree cycle

Here is a schematic of the proposed internal cycle, distinct from the spatial diagram between masses. At zero degrees, its bookkeeping is one hundred percent space energy. The percentages are postulates, not measured electron phases.

Across the first three hundred and sixty degrees, the space fraction falls as the mass fraction rises. At three hundred and sixty degrees, the illustration reaches one hundred percent mass energy.

At the mass endpoint, another incoming interaction is proposed to restart space oscillation. Across another three hundred and sixty degrees, the illustration returns to the space endpoint. The smooth curve and the triggering pulse are cartoons, not derived equations of motion.

The electron's charge must still be accounted for throughout. Calling a phase pure space does not make a charged electron into a neutral photon.

## Two turns are not a derivation of spin

In standard quantum mechanics, a spin one-half spinor changes sign under a three hundred and sixty degree rotation, and returns after seven hundred and twenty. A global sign alone is not observable; a relative sign can affect interference.

Drawing a two-turn loop does not derive that transformation law, the electron's magnetic moment, or Fermi statistics. The proposed field must reproduce those separately.

The measured electron rest energy is about five hundred and eleven kiloelectronvolts. Repartitioning a fixed internal total would not, by itself, mean the observable rest mass oscillates to zero. The model must say what a detector measures.

## The proposed center: slow, store, and restart

Now follow the author's proposed gradient inward. The high space and electromagnetic endpoint is between masses, with expansion normalized to one. The black-hole center is the opposite endpoint: zero in that component, not the highest expansion state.

The proposed center contains electrons slowing to zero as incoming interactions drive them toward maximum storage in the mass component. This is not a known highest atomic energy level. The stopped motion and the energy partition need explicit definitions and a dynamical calculation.

When another incoming photon or particle hits the stopped configuration, the proposal turns stored mass energy back into space energy and restarts oscillation. The incoming energy, recoil, charge, and any losses must also be accounted for. This is not an escape route through an event horizon.

These are the theory's proposed center dynamics, not observations of a black-hole interior. Zero electromagnetic oscillation does not establish zero total energy or zero local light speed. Standard general relativity keeps local c in regular frames; the center's physical structure remains unknown.

## One definition, two incompatible light-speed laws

The notes contain two light-speed laws. Sigma means the space-energy fraction, and u is the proposed speed divided by vacuum c. The first law grows without bound as sigma approaches one.

The bounded simulation formula instead reaches one at equal fractions and goes to zero at both endpoints. These are not equivalent laws for the same sigma.

Both give one at sigma equal to one-half. They also meet at the zero endpoint in the limiting sense. Away from those points they differ. Resolve this before treating either as a prediction, and separate local physics from coordinate effects.

## What would rule the mechanism out?

First, define the spatial space and electromagnetic scale, the internal fractions, the pressure law, and the stopped-motion variable. Derive their coupling, conserved charge, energy and momentum. Demonstrate a stable localized state and a collision-driven restart without prescribing the answer.

Second, calculate the incoming-photon budget and losses. The older galactic Larmor estimate in this repository supplies only about two point six times ten to the minus forty-four of an electron's rest energy per orbit, under that approximation. It does not test the new photon driver.

Third, compare the proposed model with quantum electrodynamics and general relativity using identical inputs. Test photon-driven, no-photon, and standard-physics controls. A scripted oscillation or a scalar-wave movie is not experimental evidence.

Finally, predict a numerical change in a magnetic moment, spectral line, scattering response, or interference signal. Declare uncertainties and exclusion criteria before examining data. A null result can reject a specified model variant, not every possible hidden mechanism.
